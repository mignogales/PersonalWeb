Preconditioning the Regression of Power Amplifier Behavioral Models and Digital Predistorters

  • ◎Regression conditioning
  • ϟDigital predistortion

This work presents insights into the regression of power amplifier behavioral models and digital predistorters. First, the least squares regression equation is split into its basic terms and these are analyzed from a correlation approach. Techniques such as normalization, whitening, coefficient selection, coefficient exclusion and Ridge regularization and their effect on the condition number are discussed. Experimental results highlight how these techniques affect the performance of the predistortion of a class J PA working in its saturation regime in a dual band scenario, concluding that the presented methods are beneficial in the regression.

Sparse Regression of Power Amplifier Behavioral Models with a Stagewise Doubly Orthogonal Matching Pursuit

  • ◎Sparse regression
  • ◇Matching pursuit

This work presents the sparse regression of Volterra-based behavioral models and digital predistorters (DPDs) through a Stagewise Doubly Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (StDOMP). First, the general sparse regression framework is presented. The StDOMP is formulated by applying the stagewise strategy to the DOMP algorithm, i.e., allowing the technique to select a block of components in each iteration without the need to perform one iteration per atom. The block selection approach allows to reduce the computational complexity up to a certain point where the performance is expected to be degraded. Experimental results highlight the behavior of the technique in the predistortion of a class AB power amplifier (PA) working in its nonlinear regime, concluding that the StDOMP is able to reduce runtime while still holding the performance level with respect to the DOMP.

Lead author Juan A. Becerra

IEEE TMTT

Canonical-Volterra-Based Model Generation for Digital Predistortion of Wideband PAs

  • ϟDigital predistortion
  • ✣Genetic algorithms

Behavioral modeling, digital predistortion, genetic algorithms, Volterra series

High-efficiency power amplifiers (PAs) exhibit pronounced nonlinear and memory effects when driven by wideband signals. Classical Volterra-based behavioral models, including generalized memory polynomial (GMP) and dynamic deviation reduction (DDR) structures, tackle the combinatorial growth of the Volterra series by imposing predefined structural constraints. However, these fixed model forms often fail to capture hardware-specific impairments, while unconstrained Volterra expansions remain computationally prohibitive. This work introduces the genetic-based Volterra-subspace generator (GVG), a novel framework that shifts the paradigm from coefficient selection within predefined models to data-driven structure discovery. The proposed approach relies on a canonical Volterra formulation in which regressors are decomposed into delayed input, conjugate, and envelope components. A genetic algorithm is employed to systematically explore the search space, evolving candidate regressors through crossover and mutation operators. The fitness of each regressor is evaluated using doubly orthogonal matching pursuit (DOMP), ensuring sparsity and numerical robustness. Unlike conventional approaches, the GVG does not impose a priori structural constraints on the model. Instead, it autonomously discovers complex cross-terms and novel basis functions tailored to the device under test, including those required to compensate for I/Q impairments. Experimental validation is conducted on three scenarios: a Doherty PA with I/Q impairments, a high-efficiency integrated PA module (PAM), and a high-power multiband (MB) PA, demonstrating that the proposed GVG technique achieves superior performance across all cases. These results demonstrate the capability of the proposed framework to identify the most relevant features without prior knowledge of the system.

Lead author Juan A. Becerra
